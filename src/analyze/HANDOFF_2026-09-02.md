## 1. Sample and conventions

- **Comparison**: GHP (full gamification) vs NG (control) only, throughout.
  gh / gp arms are excluded from all figures (2 and 1 groups respectively —
  untestable cells).
- **Outlier exclusion**: group `20260520_PM/ng1` (both market-reps). ~3× the
  volume of any other market (495 + 578 trades), price pegged at ~130 across
  both reps while v fell 120 → 8; a two-trader wash/churn pathology, not a
  bubble (PI decision, forensics in the 08-29 handoff). Exclusion is done via
  the `group_label` column now present in the processed panels
  (`EXCLUDE_GROUPS` constant in `figures.py`).
- **Resulting sample**: 10 GHP + 6 NG market-reps (5 + 3 groups), 240
  market-days, 96 trader-markets, 48 participants.
- **Group labels**: `session_id/treatment+index`, e.g. `20260520_PM/ng1`,
  built in `process_session.py` from the sorted participant set — same
  convention as the explore scripts. Panels carry `session_id` and
  `group_label`, so no session-registry lookups are needed downstream.
- **Confidence intervals in figures**: mean ± 1.96·SE across market-reps
  (n = 10 vs 6) unless stated. Reps within a group are not independent
  (8 groups): treat figure CIs as descriptive; formal tests should cluster by
  group and, given 8 clusters, use a wild cluster bootstrap
  (`fwildclusterboot`/`boottest`) before quoting borderline p-values.
- **Normalization**: v̄ = 64 (horizon-average fundamental) is used to
  normalize mispricing (RAD) and forecast errors, avoiding the mechanical
  late-day blow-up of v_t-denominated ratios (v_t falls 120 → 8).

Rebuild everything with:

```
make panels                                  # processed CSVs (data/processed/)
.venv/bin/python src/analyze/figures.py      # output/figures/fig1..fig8 (.pdf + .png)
.venv/bin/python src/analyze/error_correction.py  # output/tables/error_correction.csv
```

## 2. Critical data-alignment fact (read before touching tick data)

**MBP1 `event_seq` is a separate counter from MBO `event_seq`.** The join key
from an MBP1 book snapshot into the MBO event stream is
`source_mbo_event_seq`. Joining the two files on `event_seq = event_seq`
(as older explore code did) introduces *look-ahead*: the MBP1 counter lags the
MBO counter by the cumulative number of trade/fill records, so the "prevailing
quote before the trade" ends up being a *future* book state. Symptoms of the
bug: >50% of trades appear to print outside the quoted spread, effective
spreads of 40–80% of the mid, negative signed spreads, negative "price
impact". After correction, 95% of trades print exactly at the pre-trade best
bid or ask, and effective = realized + impact holds.

Two related facts:

- **MBP1 `trading_day` labels are unreliable** near day boundaries (inherited
  from resting orders). The pipeline remaps each MBP1 row's day from the MBO
  event it sources.
- **The aggressor side IS exported.** The `aggressor_side` column on MBO trade
  rows is empty (dropped by the exporter's schema), but the trade row's
  `side` column is derived from the engine's aggressor marker
  (`bid` = buyer-initiated). Verified: it agrees 1:1 (5,320/5,320) with
  reconstructing the aggressor as "the order whose first MBO event is an
  on-arrival fill". The 08-29 handoff's claim that trade signing is not
  computable is wrong. **Do not use Lee–Ready signing here** — it misclassifies
  a large share of trades against stale/one-sided midpoints (it produced a
  spurious order-flow imbalance of +0.5).

## 3. Variables added to the processed panels

All computed in `src/build/process_session.py` (`build_tick_metrics()` and
sections 5b/6); everything below lands in `data/processed/*_full.csv`.

### 3.1 Market-day liquidity / order flow (from MBO + MBP1)

| Column | Definition / choices |
|---|---|
| `quoted_spread`, `rel_quoted_spread` | Time-weighted over two-sided-book spells only (~85% of day time in both arms); relative = spread/midpoint. Weight = time until next snapshot. |
| `depth_best` | Time-weighted best-bid + best-ask size, two-sided spells. |
| `pct_two_sided` | Share of day time with both sides quoted. |
| `eff_spread`, `rel_eff_spread` | Per trade 2q(p − m)/m (signed, q = aggressor sign), m = midpoint of the last snapshot sourced *strictly before* the trade in MBO order; day mean. Signed rather than absolute so that effective = realized + impact holds exactly per trade; ~5% of trades execute past a just-repriced book and enter negatively. |
| `realized_spread`, `rel_realized_spread` | 2q(p − m₊₁₀ₛ)/m, q = true aggressor sign (+1 buy). m₊₁₀ₛ = prevailing two-sided mid 10 s after the trade, capped at day end. Trading days are 60 s (`trading_day_duration=1`; 99.9% of trades occur within 60 s of the day's first order — raw MBO/MBP1 event spans per day look longer only because of day-rollover admin events), and orders arrive every ~2–3 s, so 10 s allows quote adjustment without hitting the day boundary for most trades. |
| `price_impact`, `rel_price_impact` | 2q(m₊₁₀ₛ − m)/m. Identity: effective = realized + impact (signed). |
| `rv_mid` | √Σ(Δlog mid)² over two-sided snapshots, per day. |
| `volume_shares` | Σ trade size (sizes are 1; equals trade count). |
| `order_flow_imbalance` | (V_buy − V_sell)/(V_buy + V_sell) with true aggressor signs. |
| `n_limit_orders`, `n_marketable_orders`, `share_limit_orders` | An order whose first MBO event is `add` is a passive limit order; first event `fill` = marketable on arrival. Share = passive/(passive+marketable). |
| `n_cancels`, `cancel_to_order` | Cancel events; ratio to submissions. |
| `n_improving_adds`, `share_improving_adds` | Submissions that tighten the quoted spread (MBP1 snapshot spread < previous snapshot's, sourced from an add). |
| `time_to_next_order_s` | Median seconds from each trade to the next limit-order submission (either side). |
| `time_to_same_side_order_s` | Median seconds from each trade to the next limit-order submission on the CONSUMED side (the resting side the trade ate) — the sharper replenishment notion; used in fig 5C. |
| `spread_recovery_s` | Median seconds until the spread returns to ≤ its pre-trade level. |
| `avg_directionality` | Mean over active traders of \|B−S\|/(B+S) per day (1 = one-way; churn in figures = 1 − this). |

### 3.2 Trade-stream wealth reconstruction (trader panel)

**Why**: the oTree `player.num_shares` snapshot is unreliable (§4), so ALL
wealth/inequality measures in the panels are rebuilt from the trade stream —
no snapshot-based versions exist. Columns (unqualified names): trader-day
`shares`, `cash`, `wealth_day`, `rel_wealth`, `gini`; market-day
`avg_wealth`, `sd_wealth`, `gini`. The raw snapshots keep their distinct
names (`num_shares`, `current_cash`) and remain only as diagnostics.

Choices:

- Anchor: `player.assigned_initial_shares` / `assigned_initial_cash` per
  market. Reconstructed holdings sum to exactly 90 in every market-day.
- Cash applies **realized** dividends (`dividend_per_share`, a per-day draw
  from {0, 4, 8, 20}; the constant 8 in `parameters.yaml` is the
  *expectation*) to end-of-day reconstructed holdings.
- `wealth_day` = cash after the day's dividend + shares × 8 ×
  (15 − t) (remaining expected dividends). At t = 15 it equals final cash.
- Validation: for all 144 GHP/NG traders, oTree final cash − `cash`
  equals *exactly* the cumulative dividends paid on phantom shares (§4), i.e.
  the trade-stream cash accounting is exact.
- A few trader-markets have briefly **negative** reconstructed holdings
  (~30 trader-days, 40% in the excluded outlier group): the engine's
  reservation guards silently pass when a trader's runtime state is missing
  and the fill logic clamps seller holdings at zero, so limited overselling
  actually occurred. Kept as-is (double-entry consistent).

### 3.3 Regression-ready derived columns

All derived quantities used by the figures/regressions live in the panels
too (nothing is computed figure-side), so regressions can run straight off
`data/processed/`:

| Column (panel) | Definition |
|---|---|
| `rad` (market) | mean_n \|P_n − v_t\| / v̄, v̄ = 64 — PER-TRADE absolute deviation, following Asparouhova et al. (2024, RAPS); consistent with `avg_abs_mispricing` and AMR. (Stöckl et al. 2010 average the price first: recoverable as `avg_mispricing.abs()/64`.) |
| `ret_next` (market) | log(closing_{t+1}) − log(closing_t). |
| `fundamental_gap` (market) | (closing − v_t)/v̄; the error-correction regressor. |
| `churn` (both) | 1 − directionality (trader-day) / 1 − `avg_directionality` (market-day). |
| `rel_wealth` (trader) | `wealth_day` demeaned within market-day; at day 15 = final cash demeaned within market. |
| `trader_type` (trader) | Mutually exclusive label: `market_maker` first, else dominant directional type, else `other`. |
| `next_avg_price`, `forecast_err_price`, `forecast_err_fund`, `forecast_bias_fund` (trader) | Forecast vs next-day mean price / next-day fundamental, /v̄; fat-finger forecasts (> 200) set to NaN (`FORECAST_MAX_PLAUSIBLE`). |
| `above_median_literacy` (trader) | fin-quiz score above the sample-wide participant median (computed in `build_panels.py` after concatenation; currently 0.83). |

### 3.4 Trader types

- Directional flags unchanged from the original pipeline (feedback /
  speculator / fundamentalist by day-level trade direction vs price signals;
  threshold 5 days; ties split).
- **New `market_maker_flag`** (trader-market level): trades both sides of the
  book on ≥ 25% of active days AND ≥ 30 total trades (≈ one round trip per
  day). Absolute thresholds so the flag is stable across sessions; results
  are insensitive to the volume cutoff in [10, 30].
- Market-day `share_market_maker` added.
- **Presentation is mutually exclusive** (figures 6–7): "market maker" first,
  then the dominant directional type among non-MMs, then "unclassified". The
  panel flags remain atomic, so both the exclusive and the overlay views can
  be reconstructed.

## 4. Engine/data bugs discovered (matters for payoffs!)

Diagnosed in `trader_london` (engine) and `otree_trader_bridge`:

1. **`num_shares` double-counts escrowed shares.** The `/trader_info`
   endpoint returns `shares + reserved_shares`, but in the platform's
   accounting `shares` is already total ownership and `reserved_shares` is
   only a lien on open ask quantity. Hence panel share sums of 90–161 vs 90
   outstanding. The snapshot is also taken when each player's page submits
   (mid-day, asynchronous). Escrow + timing explain ~88% of snapshot values
   exactly.
2. **Dividends were paid on the inflated count** (`apply_dividend_for_day`
   pays on `shares + reserved_shares`). Real money: mean overpayment 146 E$
   per trader-market (median 74, max 1,672 — the excluded churners). This is
   embedded in `participant.payoff` / `trade_payoff` and hence in what
   subjects were actually paid. Use `cash` for economic payoffs;
   use `trade_payoff` only if you specifically want "as-paid" amounts.
3. **Limited overselling** possible via lenient reservation guards
   (see §3.2).
4. `aggressor_side` dropped by the export schema but preserved in the trade
   `side` column (§2).

**Fix (1)–(3) in the engine before the next data collection wave.**

## 6. Main findings, in the order the mechanism runs

1. **Activity**: GHP doubles volume with the same passive/aggressive order
   mix; a third of GHP traders behave as (entertainment) market makers doing
   half the volume; churn ↑.
2. **Liquidity**: quoted/effective spreads halve; price impact falls ~7×;
   depth and order composition unchanged → improvement comes from
   undercutting competition and fast replenishment (fig 5), i.e. *price* of
   liquidity, not quantity.
3. **No rents**: MMs break even; realized spreads ≈ 0 in GHP (vs −16% in NG,
   where posting quotes loses to adverse repricing). Liquidity provision is
   level-agnostic churn.
4. **Error correction switches off**: NG closes ~60–65% of the fundamental
   gap per day; GHP ~none (interaction +0.48, t 2.4). In flow terms: NG's
   aggressive side is fundamentalists dumping inventory (net −102 ex-MM);
   in GHP that same group flips to net aggressive *buyers* (+46).
5. **Why nobody leans (dynamic channel)**: the daily carry of holding an
   overpriced share (≈ +2–3 E$ = 8 dividend − ~5 price decline) is positive
   in GHP in *every* window, so riding dominates myopically; NG's carry goes
   negative mid-game (−3), triggering the corrective selling. Late exit is
   rationed anyway (75% of late asks never fill). GHP fundamentalists hold
   ~15 shares late and even accumulate (14 → 20 by day 15).
6. **Beliefs ratify it** (fig 7): GHP traders are the *better* next-day price
   forecasters and increasingly expect prices above fundamentals — expecting
   the bubble to persist is correct, and it is why not leaning is rational.
7. **Distribution**: payoff spread across types widens in GHP
   (fundamentalists +~300, feedback −~300, unclassified worst); literacy
   median-split and Gini levels are weak at GHP-vs-NG (consistent with 08-29
   nulls). Dividend overpayments (§4) accrued to whoever parked big asks —
   corrected payoffs (`cash`) compress the raw differences.
