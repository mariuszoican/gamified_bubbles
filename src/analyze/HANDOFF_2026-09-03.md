## 1. Sample and conventions

- **Comparison**: GHP (full gamification) vs NG (control) only, throughout.
  gh / gp arms are excluded from all figures and tables (2 and 1 groups —
  untestable cells).
- **Outlier exclusion**: group `20260520_PM/ng1` (both market-reps). ~3× the
  volume of any other market (495 + 578 trades), price pegged at ~130 across
  both reps while v fell 120 → 8; a two-trader wash/churn pathology, not a
  bubble (PI decision, forensics in the 08-29 handoff). Exclusion is via
  `group_label` (`EXCLUDE_GROUPS` in `figures.py` and `regressions.R`).
- **Resulting sample**: 10 GHP + 6 NG market-reps (5 + 3 groups), 240
  market-days, 96 trader-markets, 48 participants.
- **Group labels**: `session_id/treatment+index`, e.g. `20260520_PM/ng1`.
- **Figure CIs**: mean ± 1.96·SE across market-reps (n = 10 vs 6) unless
  stated. Reps within a group are not independent (8 groups): treat figure
  CIs as descriptive.
- **Regression SEs**: clustered by experimental **group** (`group_label`)
  throughout. The group dimension binds (treatment is assigned at the
  group; two-way group × day was tried and does not change inference).
  With only 8 clusters, CR1 SEs are anti-conservative — confirm borderline
  p-values with a wild cluster bootstrap (`fwildclusterboot` from
  `https://s3alfisc.r-universe.dev`) before quoting.
- **Normalization**: v̄ = 64 (horizon-average fundamental) for RAD, the
  error-correction gap, and forecast errors. Avoids the mechanical late-day
  blow-up of v_t-denominated ratios (v_t falls 120 → 8).

Rebuild:

```
make panels
.venv/bin/python src/analyze/figures.py
Rscript src/analyze/regressions.R
.venv/bin/python src/analyze/error_correction.py   # csv companion to T8
```

## 2. Critical data-alignment fact (read before touching tick data)

**MBP1 `event_seq` is a separate counter from MBO `event_seq`.** The join key
from an MBP1 book snapshot into the MBO event stream is
`source_mbo_event_seq`. Joining on `event_seq = event_seq` introduces
look-ahead: the "prevailing quote before the trade" is a *future* book
state. Symptoms: >50% of trades print outside the quoted spread, effective
spreads of 40–80% of the mid, negative signed spreads / price impact.
After correction, 95% of trades print at the pre-trade best bid or ask,
and effective = realized + impact holds.

- **MBP1 `trading_day` labels are unreliable** near day boundaries. The
  pipeline remaps each MBP1 row's day from the sourced MBO event.
- **The aggressor side IS exported.** `aggressor_side` on MBO trade rows is
  empty (exporter schema), but the trade row's `side` column is the
  engine's aggressor marker (`bid` = buyer-initiated). Verified 1:1
  (5,320/5,320) against "first MBO event is an on-arrival fill". **Do not
  use Lee–Ready** — it produced a spurious OFI of +0.5 against stale /
  one-sided mids.

## 3. Variables (all in `process_session.py` → `data/processed/`)

Nothing plotted or regressed is computed figure-side. Full column list is
in the 09-02 handoff §3; the notes below are the ones that still trip
people up.

### 3.1 Liquidity / order flow

Time-weighted quoted spread and depth on two-sided spells only. Effective /
realized / impact use the true aggressor `side` and the last MBP1 snapshot
sourced *strictly before* the trade (`source_mbo_event_seq`). Realized
horizon is **10 s** (days are 60 s). `order_flow_imbalance` is signed
(V_buy − V_sell)/(V_buy + V_sell); tables that want one-sidedness use
`abs_order_flow_imbalance`. Replenishment in fig 5 / T4 is
`time_to_same_side_order_s` (next limit order on the *consumed* side), not
`time_to_next_order_s`.

### 3.2 Wealth

All `shares` / `cash` / `wealth_day` / `rel_wealth` / `gini` are
trade-stream reconstructions. oTree `num_shares` double-counts escrow and
is not used. `rel_wealth` is within-market demeaned; at day 15 it is final
economic cash minus the market mean. `trade_payoff` is "as-paid" (includes
phantom dividends on escrowed shares) — do not use it for economics.

### 3.3 Mispricing

Per-trade convention (Asparouhova et al. 2024): `avg_abs_mispricing` =
mean_n |P_n − v_t|, AMR = that / v_t, RAD = that / v̄. Stöckl-style
"|mean price − v| / v̄" is recoverable as `avg_mispricing.abs()/64` and
is **not** what we plot or regress.

### 3.4 Trader types — read before interpreting fig 6 / T5 / T5b

Directional flags (day-level):

- feedback: `net_position × (P_{t−1} − P_{t−2}) > 0`
- speculator: `net_position × (P_{t+1} − P_t) > 0`
- fundamentalist: `net_position × (P_t − v_t) < 0`  (lean against that day's mispricing)

`net_position` = n_buys − n_sells **that day**. Flags are summed over 15
days; a trader-market is classified if the winning count ≥ 5
(`trader_type_threshold`). Ties split. **Market maker** is assigned first
(both sides on ≥ 25% of active days and ≥ 30 trades), then the dominant
directional type among non-MMs, else `other`.

This is a **plurality of days**, not a statement about inventory or
impact. A GHP "fundamentalist" has ~7.7 lean days (just above the
threshold) but is a **net buyer** over the market (+7 shares, final
holdings ~22). Sell-when-overpriced is only ~28% of their days; the rest
of the flags are buy-when-P < v. Small sells on many days beat large
rides on a few days in the day-count, so the label survives while they
do not close the gap. Do not read T5b or fig 6C as "fundamentalists
correct prices."

## 6. Regression tables (`src/analyze/regressions.R` → `output/tables/`)

`ols()` wraps `feols(..., cluster = ~group_label)`. FE are expanded as
dummies and **centered at their NG means** (and re-centered on complete
cases of the outcome), so the intercept is the **unconditional
non-gamified mean** of the estimation sample. Slopes on `gamified` are
the same as absorbed FE. In interacted specs (`gap × gamified`,
`late × gamified`) the main `Gamified` coefficient is the treatment
effect at the NG mean of the other regressor, not at zero.

| Table | File | Spec |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `t1_mispricing.tex` | AMR, RAD, abs. mispricing; each with rep dummy, then rep + day. |
| 2 | `t2_liquidity.tex` | Quoted / effective / realized / impact / depth / RV; full FE. |
| 3 | `t3_volume_orderflow.tex` | Trades, \|OFI\|, churn, limit orders, share limit; full FE. |
| 4 | `t4_liquidity_provision.tex` | Improving adds, same-side replenishment, spread recovery; each with day FE then day + rep. |

## 7. Mechanism

Reduced form (already H1, first channel): hedonic gamification manufactures
noise-like volume → the book looks more liquid → prices carry less of \(v\).
That is Bloomfield, O'Hara and Saar (2009) / Chapkovski (2024), not the
slogan "more liquidity ⇒ faster discovery."

It is **not** the Kyle / Glosten–Milgrom filter. \(v\) is common knowledge
(\(v_t = 8 \times\) remaining dividends). In those models noise lowers
\(\lambda\) and an informed residual is still in the tape; cheaper impact
would make leaning *easier*. Here T8's `Gap × Gamified` survives OFI — the
signed flow is not a drowned fundamental signal. There is no informed
residual. The people producing the liquidity **are** the noise.

Who that is (T5): MM volume share +38 pp, unclassified −19 pp; directional
shares flat. Entertainment two-sided traders, not Haruvy feedback (H1's
second channel is the weaker one in GHP vs NG). The type label `other`
("noise" in the hypothesis write-up) *shrinks*; Kyle-noise shows up as MMs
quoting both sides around the current mid. Gross MM trades +9.3 (n.s.);
fundamentals do not trade less.

What "higher liquidity" is: **resilience, not depth**. Depth at best is
unchanged (T2, ~2.6). You can eat the posted size; someone puts it back at
the same mid (T4: same-side refill ~2.5 s faster, spread recovery ~9 s
faster; more improving adds). Volume ~2×, churn up, \(|OFI|\) 0.74 → 0.43
(T3). Realized spreads ≈ 0 in GHP. Hang the liquidity paragraph on T3/T4,
not on T2 quoted/impact (unsigned).

Abreu and Brunnermeier (2003, *Econometrica*) have no spread or dealer.
They have a reduced-form absorption constraint: the bubble bursts only when
aggregate selling pressure exceeds a threshold \(\kappa\); each arb is
position-constrained, so one seller cannot dislodge the price. GHP MM
refill is that \(\kappa\) in the lab — informal mapping, not a claim that
AB derive "tighter quotes ⇒ worse bubbles." DSSW (1990) is a cousin
("noise can prevent convergence"), not the mechanism: their noise is a
belief shock about \(v\), with a risk premium. Here \(v\) is known and the
reason not to lean is carry plus a standing exit, not resale risk.

Incentive, keep the two "try"s apart:

- **Try = move \(P\) to \(v\)** (an attack): futile. One sell is absorbed;
  the mid does not stay where it was put. T4 at the quote, T8 at daily
  frequency (NG closes ~70% of the gap per day; GHP ~12–15%).
- **Try = get out**: easy. A refilled bid is a standing exit. Volume is
  up and effective spreads are down — you *can* consume liquidity. You
  just cannot dislodge the quote.

So subjects do not fail to sell because they cannot. They hold because
selling now does not crash the bubble, the book will still be there
tomorrow, and overnight carry stays \(\geq 0\) after day 4 (fig 10: drop
vs \(E[D]=8\)). NG is the opposite: the drop spikes above 8 around days
6–7, the book does not refill fast enough, and selling continues until
the path locks onto \(v\) (fig 9). Forecasts agree (fig 7): GHP subjects
are better at next-day *price* and correctly expect \(P > v\).

The "fundamentalist" label is a day-count (§3.4), not inventory. In GHP
they are net buyers. Do not write them as error-correctors.

This is a **joint treatment effect**, not a mediation of liquidity onto
the bubble. T1 levels are unsigned (8 clusters); the bubble claim lives
in fig 9 and T8. Confirm T3 trades and T8 `Gap × Gamified` with a wild
cluster bootstrap before citing stars.
